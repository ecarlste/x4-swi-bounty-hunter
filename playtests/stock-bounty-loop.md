### Verdict

**Draft for the player to correct (2026-11-05 14:56).** Sources: the hunt log, the notes per hunt, and the save reads in research/save-check-*.md. Totals are from the logged figures; the logbook extractor's cross-check is in [research/playtest-logbook-end.md](../research/playtest-logbook-end.md).

**Headline (player, 2026-11-05 14:54):** the stock bounty system works, but it's a skeleton that "was never given a fully fleshed out design and was just added to be there". Making it "super fun and action packed" is the extension's job. Its best moment, the hunter as assassin who gets in, kills the target and gets out (hunts 1 and 13), comes from the sandbox, not from the mission's design.

- **Variety: it repeats, and the variety that exists is emergent.** There's one contract type (Search and destroy), and 16 hunts ran the same steps: dock at a BHG station, accept, fly to a stale position, scan, kill, fly to a debrief. Targets repeat: 6 Weequays, 3 Gozantis, 2 Y-wings. What made hunts different came from the sandbox, not the mission:
  - the target's wing: 3 B-wings (hunt 5), 3 ARC-170s (hunt 12), a 10-ship fleet (hunt 13);
  - disguises: 3 of 16 hunts (8, 10, 13);
  - rival hunters: Boba Fett and Nobutora (hunt 6), a Razor Crest (Gizer);
  - third parties: a New Republic X-wing stole hunt 9, and an Anoat patrol was the distraction in hunt 13;
  - battlefields worth looting.

  The board can't tell these hunts apart in advance. "Medium" covered all of them, so the variety arrives as a surprise, not as a choice.
- **Pay: bounties alone aren't a living until you can take veterans; loot out-earns them.**
  - **Bounty pay:** 12 paid hunts, **5,140,431 Cr** (confirmed by the logbook extractor), over 13.1 game hours of play (12.0 from the first accept to the last debrief), about 390-430k per game hour. Four hunts failed or were dropped (3, 5, 7, 9), and hunt 10's first run paid 0.
  - **In the Fortitude** (hunts 1-7), the hunts it could win paid 93-354k each, about 0.8 M in all. Veterans and turreted Ms were out of reach.
  - **In R-41 wings, then the HWK-290:** plain targets paid 93-440k. Veterans paid 0.91-1.77 M (hunts 12 and 13), the best money in the loop.
  - **Loot out-earned the bounties.** About 6.9 M Cr in cash pickups plus about 2.2 M in salvage and item sales. Looting a big battle took 5-8 min, against 20-30 min for a hunt. The player switched to scavenging "per time invested" (2026-11-04 16:11).
  - **Ship ladder:**
    - Fortitude → 3 R-41s on day 2, paid for by selling a captured Y-wing and the Fortitude plus the hunt money;
    - → 4 R-41s;
    - → HWK-290 on day 3, needing the 3 M Guild ship licence plus 1.86 M, and paid for mostly by loot;
    - → 3 HWK-290s + 4 R-41s.

    Bounty money alone wouldn't have bought the hunter's signature ship. On top of that, the damage rule took a hidden cut: hunts 8 and 10 lost 56-67% of the destruction pay to damage dealt before the scan.
- **Pacing: a hunt is 20-30 min, and most of it is travel.** Once the player hunted near Milagro, accept to turn-in ran about 22-23 min (hunts 8 and 11). The search was often near zero: hunt 10's 3 h 43 min report was found in about 5 min. Fights lasted a few minutes. The rest was flying to the target, to a debrief elsewhere and back to a BHG station. The board can't be used over comms, so every hunt starts with a dock.

  Dead time and friction:
  - hunt 7's 25 min to Kalee;
  - the early game, about 1 h 20 min to the first hunt;
  - silent failures (hunts 3 and 9);
  - a 3 h limit hidden behind "Time left: -".

  That gives two or three hunts per real hour, with a few minutes of action each. For an episode, the best hunts (1, 12, 13) have a clear arc: find, size up the wing, plan, strike, escape. The loop around them is errands.
- **Reputation and the sanction:** the player picked pirate targets to avoid reputation losses. Every wing kill counts as an Unauthorised Kill (hunt 12: CSA +1 → -8), and Black Sun reached -11 (no docking) from pirate hunts alone. The sanction doesn't fit how hunts actually go: escorts are never covered, and a wingman's kill on the leader isn't either ([research/sanction-tiers.md](../research/sanction-tiers.md)). The player bought a Partial sanction once (hunt 16, 34,960 Cr) and it did nothing on a pirate target.
- **Solo vs. fleet (player, 2026-11-05 14:26):** solo hunting only works against fighters you can kill before their wing joins in. The stock loop pushes the player into a fleet.
- **What the extension must add before episode 1** (input for [Extension scope at launch](https://github.com/ecarlste/x4-swi-bounty-hunter/issues/11)):
  - **One bounty-hunter window, reachable from anywhere (player requirement, 2026-11-03):** a single UI for everything bounty-hunter related: contacts, offers, accepted hunts, debriefs. Open at all times, not only when docked at a wharf. In-fiction reason: it's a starship with a hyperdrive in Star Wars, so the hunter should be able to call any station that isn't jammed (by a battle, say), and above all contacts already met and done business with. This answers several stock-loop pains from this session: flying to a wharf to see a board, docking to debrief (T04), the menu under "... more. (Mods)" (T03), and a silently failed accept with no feedback (see the hunt 3 notes).
  - **The hourly accept limit (player requirement, 2026-11-03):** either prevent the bug (an accept that fails still uses one of the three slots) or let the player turn the limit off with a setting in the window. The limit is SWI's: 3 accepts per game hour, the cap at EM 5097, counted at EM 5582 ([research/missing-accepted-hunt.md](../research/missing-accepted-hunt.md)). Options to weigh in #11: count an accept only once the mission exists; a setting for the cap (off, or a number); or both.
  - **The starting ship (player, 2026-11-04):** the Fortitude isn't a good bounty-hunter ship to begin with (an M gunboat: no missiles, out-turned and outrun by fighters), so even "easy" S hunts are an uphill fight. It worked on lone or lightly escorted targets (hunts 1, 2, 4) and failed on a veteran with three B-wings (hunt 5). Question for #11 and the story (#3): give the hunter a path to a proper hunting ship early, or offer hunts matched to what the player flies.
    - The Fortitude can't mount ion cannons or missiles (player, 2026-11-04), so no refit closes the gap: hunts like 5 need a different ship.
    - It also handles too badly to dodge turrets (hunt 7: "flies like a semi truck"), so even a lone M gunship like a Gozanti is out of reach. The usual way to kill an M is an agile S fighter.
    - **The ship the player wants is the HWK-290**, but the Bounty Hunters Guild sells it only with a licence. The guild's licences are 1 M + 3 M Cr; the HWK needs only the 3 M one ([research/licence-costs.md](../research/licence-costs.md), agent read 2026-11-04). Plus the ship, about 1.5 M Cr with the high loadout preset: about 4.5 M Cr in all. Bounty work so far has paid about 644 k Cr (hunts 1, 2, 4) in roughly 5 h of real time. At that rate the licence alone takes about 23 hours of play, and the whole 4.5 M about 35. The Pirate Syndicates also sell the HWK-290 (1 M licence, about 2 M in all), but the player starts at the hostile edge with them, and the HWK carries no missiles anyway.
    - **Cheaper hunting ships (same read):** with about 0.5 M Cr, an R-41 Starchaser (219 k; the player already has its licence) takes ion cannons and a concussion missile launcher, but has a quarter of the Fortitude's hull. An X-wing T65B (255 k) is the alternative. Next step up: Kamino's 1 M licence plus an ARC-170, about 1.25 M. Corporations sell military ship licences with no reputation or citizenship: Kamino 1 M; Sienar, Rendili, Incom, Mon Cal 2 M; TransGalMeg 3 M; Kuat, Corellia, Hoersch-Kessel 5 M. So a missile-armed fighter is within reach now; it's the hunter's signature ship that's tens of hours away. Not yet tried in play. The player's read: **it feels like you'd need non-BH work to get to the point where you can do BH work.** That breaks the rule this playtest set (money from bounty work only) as soon as a player wants a proper ship.
      - **Correction from play (player, 11:19):** a licence isn't enough. To buy a ship the player also needs enough reputation with a faction whose shipyard sells it; the alternative is capturing one that was abandoned or whose pilot ejected. So even the 219k R-41 means a reputation grind (non-bounty work again) or boarding/capture luck. The licence read (research/licence-costs.md) didn't cover the shipyard's reputation requirement.
  - **Early game: you can barely take targets, and can't tell which (player, 2026-11-04 11:38):** in the starting ship most hunts are at or past the edge of what the player can win, and nothing tells you how hard one will actually be. "Difficulty" says Medium for both a lone Weequay (hunts 2, 6) and a veteran Y-wing with three missile-armed B-wings (hunt 5). The offer leaves out the wing, the escort ships and the target's loadout; the kills line only hints (a .5 means a subordinate). The player finds out by flying there and fighting, and a wrong guess costs the ship (hunt 5) or a 60k repair (hunt 6). The extension should show what the hunter would know: the wing, the ships, a threat rating against the player's own ship.
    - **And no good way to earn your way up (player, 11:43):** the money to become able isn't there either. The hunts you can win pay 125-354k, minus repairs (60k after hunt 6); a better ship needs a shipyard's reputation as well as a licence, or a lucky capture; the police licence needs a station first. So the early game is a loop with no exit: too weak for most hunts, and no bounty-hunter way to stop being too weak. For #11: the extension needs a ramp, e.g. small hunts matched to the starting ship that pay toward the next ship, or a guild path to a proper hunting ship.
    - **The player's answer (11:44): one window plus real intel would make progress feasible.** If every bounty were in one place, the whole bounty-hunter playthrough managed from one UI, and each offer showed how strong the target actually is and its escort, the player could pick the hunts the ship can win and skip the rest. That alone would turn the loop into steady progress, without changing the pay. This ties the two earlier requirements (the bounty-hunter window, and wing intel on the offer) to the ramp problem: the money exists in the winnable hunts; the stock UI just hides which ones they are.
    - **The ship ladder should run through corporations (player, 11:46):** the blocker is SWI's station requirement (citizenship needs owned production stations) on the military path to better ships; without it the player thinks the loop would be fine. But the extension's goal is a fun bounty-hunter-only playstyle, so it shouldn't ask the player to build stations. So the ladder to larger ships likely runs through the corporations, which sell licences with no reputation or citizenship ([research/licence-costs.md](../research/licence-costs.md): Kamino 1 M; Sienar, Rendili, Incom, Mon Cal 2 M; TransGalMeg 3 M; Kuat, Corellia, Hoersch-Kessel 5 M). **Open:** whether a corporation's shipyard also needs reputation to buy from (the 11:19 correction says faction shipyards do), and which bounty-hunter ships each corporation sells at each size.
      - **Corporation diplomacy screens (player, 11:50; `research/screenshots/corp-licences-*.png`):** both at Neutral (+0). Corellian Engineering (CEC): civilian equipment and ships licences held from Enemy (-10), Military Equipment Access 100,000 Cr, Military Ships Access 5,000,000 Cr, then Friend (+10), Investor (Ally +20). Sienar (SIE): the same civilian licences, then a longer ladder: Basic Construction 100k, Military Equipment Access 250k, Intermediate Construction 1 M, Capital Equipment Access 1 M, Advanced Construction 2 M, Military Ships Access 2 M, Capital Ships Access 10 M, Starfighter Facilities 10 M, Shipyard Facilities 40 M. So corporations differ: some offer capital ships and building paths, some don't. **From the code (agent read, 12:06; [research/corp-ship-ladder.md](../research/corp-ship-ladder.md); untested in play):** a Neutral player can buy from corporations. Every corporation licence needs only -10, the shop checks only the licence of the station's owner (the corporation, not the sector's government), and no ship ware has a minrelation; the player starts at 0 with all corporations. Corporations, the Guild, pirates, Rebels, Sith and Naboo have no citizenship requirement. Bad relations with governments only matter on the way there (at -20 their military attacks combat ships in Scipio, Geonosis, Corellia, Kuat and Rendili). Construction and Facilities licences only sell station-module blueprints; a hunter doesn't need them. Two ladders: **no licence**, all at TransGalMeg in Mataou: R-41 → Weequay Corvette → Interceptor Frigate, about 7.2 M in all, with no ion weapons after the R-41; **with ion weapons**: Kamino ship licence (1 M) + ARC-170 → TransGalMeg Weequay → Kamino equipment licence (500 k) + Pelta II or Arquitens, about 9.9 M. So the corporate route avoids the reputation wall of the 11:19 correction: an R-41 should be buyable now, at TransGalMeg in Mataou.
      - **Kamino as the hunter's supplier (player, 12:08; story idea for #3 and concept.md):** the ion-weapon ladder starting at Kamino is a good lore tie-in: Jango Fett worked with the Kaminoans and lived on Kamino, and Boba Fett spent part of his childhood there. A hunter buying ships and gear from Kamino fits the "feel like Boba Fett" bar. Kamino is also very close to where the Bounty Hunter start begins (player, 12:11), so it works as the first upgrade stop without a long trip. Distances from Bothawui (where the player thinks the start began, 12:15): **Kamino 2 jumps**, TransGalMeg in **Mataou about 12 jumps**. So the no-licence R-41 route means a long trip, while the Kamino route is next door but costs a 1 M licence first.
  - **The bar (player, 2026-11-03):** bounty hunting in SWI should feel like being Boba Fett: the hunter has the contacts, the intel and the ship, and the work comes to them. The stock loop's flying to boards and docking to debrief feels like running errands.
  - **A tracking minigame for the search (player, 2026-11-03):** finding the target needs more of a tracking game that keeps the player engaged and having fun, not flying around pulsing long-range scans at a stale position (hunt 5: about 25 minutes with nothing found). Ideas to weigh: leads that narrow the search (sightings from stations, informants, traffic that saw it pass), fresher last-seen reports as you get closer, trails to follow (ion trails, jump records at gates), and the wing as part of the intel. The shape the player wants: you keep moving toward clues you find along the way, each one leading to the next, ideally lore-friendly (Star Wars ways a hunter finds a mark: informants, bribed dock officials, hyperspace route records, a tracking fob from the client).

Candidates seen so far: SWI's Emergent Missions hunts shown and accepted from the window (offers, pay, accept feedback, remote debrief); a wanted board with last-seen intel, including the target's wing ([research/headhunter.md](../research/headhunter.md) §8); general kill bounties away from a wharf (§2); wanted-ship-nearby alerts (§9-10); contacts who remember you.
