# YouTube channel: revive Vamorix or start a new one (2026-11-02)

Labels: **[Official]** = YouTube Help, YouTube Blog, or a YouTube employee speaking on Creator Insider. **[Reported official]** = a YouTube statement we only have through trade-press coverage (we could not play the video, or found no YouTube-hosted copy). **[Unsourced]** = a claim the research didn't keep a source for; check it before relying on it. **[Folklore]** = creator or blog advice with no YouTube source behind it.

The plan this is for ([concept.md](../concept.md#format)): about 4 h a week of sessions in fixed slots (2×1 h weekday, 1×2 h weekend), recorded for the whole first run, with live streaming perhaps in a later run. Each week gives one 10-20 min story video as a Premiere, with extras banked. Raw recordings stay up, and the mod gets showcase videos. No Shorts.

## 1. Does a dormant channel's history hurt new videos?

- **[Reported official] Inactive subscribers don't count against you.** In a 2020 Q&A video from YouTube's discovery team ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v4qXzri4rd0), "5 Search and Discovery Questions Answered by YouTube Employees!"), YouTube said "inactive subscribers is not a factor impacting YouTube's recommendation algorithm" and "will not impact overall distribution". Inactive viewers just see your uploads less often ([SEJ summary](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-recommendation-algorithm/387030/), [Social Media Today summary](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-answers-common-questions-on-video-discovery-and-distribution/588415/)).
- **[Reported official] YouTube judges each video on its own, not the channel as a whole.** The same Q&A says YouTube "doesn't make assessments about a channel as a whole based on the performance of a few videos". So old, low-view videos don't drag down new ones.
- **[Reported official] A new channel is only for a new direction.** The same Q&A says "creating a new channel and re-uploading the same videos will not help". It suggests a new channel only when you take "a different direction with your content".
- **[Reported official] Match channels to audiences.** Todd Beaupré, YouTube's discovery and growth lead, spoke on Creator Insider on 2026-09-01 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rHLjxrbXmmY), reported by [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)). His advice was to "align channels with audiences": if the same people would watch both, use one channel; if not, separate brands set clearer expectations. Most recommendations are "pulled for each viewer" when the viewer opens YouTube, and reach non-subscribers early. Subscribing "isn't as strong as a lot of people think". A new topic takes time to find its audience, so don't compare new videos with the channel's old best video. The line often quoted from him, "Same audience? Same channel. Different audience? Different channel.", appears only second-hand ([Panda Video](https://www.pandavideo.com/blog/shorts-and-long-form-videos-same-channel), which cites vidIQ).
- **[Reported official] Inactive subscribers stop getting push notifications.** Tested in 2025, rolled out in April 2026. Subscribers with the bell on "All" who haven't watched in about a month stop getting push alerts but still get inbox notices; pushes resume once they watch again ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-stops-push-notifications-for-inactive-subscribers-in-new-rollout/); we found no YouTube-hosted announcement). A dead subscriber list just goes quiet.
- **[Official] Subscriber counts.** YouTube removes closed and spam accounts from subscriber counts, and has said this "will in no way affect your views" ([YouTube Blog, 2012](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/upcoming-improvements-to-accuracy-of/)).
- **[Unsourced] Notifications are capped.** Each viewer gets at most 3 new-video notifications per channel in 24 h, but there's no cap on how many of a channel's videos YouTube recommends. If it holds, a week with several uploads (raw recordings, story video, showcase) should spread them out and skip notifications on the raw recordings.
- **[Folklore]** Advice that a revival needs "500+ subs and under 2 years dormant", and that an old audience of a different niche "confuses the algorithm". No YouTube source backs either ([example](https://primalvideo.com/guides/how-to-revive-a-dead-youtube-channel/), [example](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-questions/should-you-revive-an-old-youtube-channel-or-start-a-new-one/)). We found no official statement on whether a former audience's taste slows the first impressions a new topic gets; Beaupré's "takes time" remark hints at a slow start, not a penalty.

## 2. Revive or new channel: renames and old videos

**[Official] Renaming is cheap.** The channel name and the handle can each change twice in 14 days. Changing the name removes a verification badge ([Help: manage channel name/handle](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2657964)). Custom URLs can no longer be created or changed; links go to the handle-based URL ([Help: channel URLs](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2657968)). So Vamorix could take a space-games name (e.g. "Spaceman Vam") with a new handle. The Help page doesn't say whether the old handle is held or freed; [Folklore] reports say YouTube holds it for a short while.

**[Official] Hiding old videos.** Private and unlisted videos drop out of search, recommendations, subscriber feeds and the channel's Videos tab. Unlisted videos stay viewable by link and can sit in a public playlist; private ones can't ([Help: privacy settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/157177)). We found no official statement that unlisting or privating hurts a channel. Our inference: since YouTube judges each video on its own (section 1), there's no obvious way it could. Privating is reversible; deleting isn't.

## 3. Raw recordings next to edited videos; live-stream requirements

**Mixing long raw recordings with edited videos.** We found **no** official guidance telling creators to put streams or raw recordings on a separate channel, and no statement that they hurt edited uploads. What applies is section 1: each video is judged on its own, and recommendations are pulled per viewer. Beaupré (2026) adds that long videos are served where people watch them, e.g. on TVs. The audience test is the tie-breaker: people who watch 2-hour raw sessions and people who watch 15-minute story cuts may be different groups, the only official reason to split. **[Folklore]** Many streamers run a second "VODs" channel to keep the main page tidy and keep low-retention uploads off the notification slots (section 1). The simpler fix on one channel: a playlist of their own, and no notifications when uploading a raw recording.

**[Official] Live-stream requirements** ([Help: intro to live streaming](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2474026), [Help: mobile live](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9228390), [Help: webcam live](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2853700)):

- Verify the channel (phone verification), with no live-streaming restrictions in the past 90 days. Minimum age 16.
- "Starting your first live stream may take up to 24 hours" after turning on live streaming; after that you can go live right away.
- Mobile live needs at least 50 subscribers. Desktop (an encoder such as OBS, or a webcam) has no subscriber minimum, and gameplay from a PC uses the encoder route.

## Recommendation (superseded)

Written before the decision. On the official evidence, reviving Vamorix has no algorithmic penalty and probably skips the 24 h live wait. A new channel is a choice about branding and audience, not the algorithm, and costs only that wait and a second channel to manage. Either way: raw recordings in their own playlist without notifications, and uploads spread across the week (section 1).

## Decision (2026-11-02)

A new space-games channel (name to be picked) under the same Google account. Vamorix stays as it is, with its old videos public: the creator won't private or unlist them. It points to the new channel with a featured channel link, or a community post if that tab is on. The first run is recorded, so the 24 h live wait doesn't matter. See [concept.md](../concept.md#format).
