# YouTube channel: revive Vamorix or start "Spaceman Vam"?

Researched 2026-11-02. Labels: **[Official]** = YouTube Help, YouTube Blog, or a YouTube
employee speaking on Creator Insider. **[Reported official]** = a YouTube statement we
only have through trade-press coverage (we could not play the video). **[Folklore]** =
creator or blog advice with no YouTube source behind it.

The plan this is for: a fixed schedule of about 4 h/week recorded (2x1h weekday + 1x2h
weekend). The first sessions are recorded, later ones go live. That gives 1-2 edited 10-20 min story videos a week,
full raw VODs kept up, and mod showcase videos. No Shorts.

## 1. Does a dormant channel's history hurt new videos?

- **[Reported official] Inactive subscribers do not count against you.** In a 2020
  Q&A video from YouTube's discovery team
  ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v4qXzri4rd0), "5 Search and Discovery
  Questions Answered by YouTube Employees!"), YouTube said "inactive subscribers is not a
  factor impacting YouTube's recommendation algorithm" and "will not impact overall
  distribution". Inactive viewers just see your uploads less often.
  ([SEJ summary](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-recommendation-algorithm/387030/),
  [Social Media Today summary](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-answers-common-questions-on-video-discovery-and-distribution/588415/))
- **[Reported official] YouTube judges each video on its own, not the channel as a whole.** The same Q&A says YouTube
  "doesn't make assessments about a channel as a whole based on the performance of a
  few videos". So old, low-view videos do not drag down new ones.
- **[Reported official] Start a new channel only for a new direction.** The same Q&A says
  "creating a new channel and re-uploading the same videos will not help". YouTube
  suggests a new channel only when you take "a different direction with your content".
- **[Reported official] Match channels to audiences.** Todd Beaupré, YouTube's discovery and growth
  lead, spoke on Creator Insider on 2026-09-01
  ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rHLjxrbXmmY), reported by
  [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)).
  His advice was to "align channels with audiences": if the same people would watch both,
  use one channel; if not, separate brands set clearer expectations. Most recommendations
  are "pulled for each viewer" when the viewer opens YouTube, and reach non-subscribers
  early. He also said subscribing "isn't as strong as a lot of people think". He warned that a
  new topic takes time to find its audience, so don't compare new videos with the
  channel's old best video. A line often quoted from him, "Same audience? Same channel.
  Different audience? Different channel.", appears only second-hand
  ([Panda Video](https://www.pandavideo.com/blog/shorts-and-long-form-videos-same-channel),
  which cites vidIQ).
- **[Official] Inactive subscribers stop getting push notifications.** YouTube first tested this in
  2025 and rolled it out in April 2026. Subscribers with the bell on "All" who haven't watched in about a month stop
  getting push alerts, but still get inbox notices. Pushes resume once they watch again
  ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-stops-push-notifications-for-inactive-subscribers-in-new-rollout/);
  we found no YouTube-hosted link to the announcement). The upshot: a dead subscriber list
  just goes quiet. It doesn't count against you.
- **[Official] Subscriber counts.** YouTube removes closed and spam accounts from
  subscriber counts, and has said this "will in no way affect your views"
  ([YouTube Blog, 2012](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/upcoming-improvements-to-accuracy-of/)).
- **[Reported official] Notifications are capped.** Each viewer gets at most 3 new-video notifications per
  channel in 24 h, but there is no cap on how many of a channel's videos YouTube recommends. This
  matters if a weekly drop includes several uploads at once (raw VOD, story video,
  showcase): spread them out.
- **[Folklore]** Advice that a revival needs "500+ subs and under 2 years dormant", and that
  an old audience of a different niche "confuses the algorithm". No YouTube source backs either
  ([example](https://primalvideo.com/guides/how-to-revive-a-dead-youtube-channel/),
  [example](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-questions/should-you-revive-an-old-youtube-channel-or-start-a-new-one/)).
  The official line says old subscribers simply go quiet. What we found no
  official statement on: whether a former audience's *taste profile* slows the first
  impressions a new topic gets. Beaupré's "takes time" remark hints at a slow start, not a
  penalty.

## 2. Revive or new channel: renames and old videos

**[Official] Renaming is cheap.** You can change the channel name twice in 14 days, and
the handle twice in 14 days. Changing the name removes a verification badge, which doesn't matter
here ([Help: manage channel name/handle](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2657964)).
Custom URLs can no longer be created or changed, and links now go to the
handle-based URL ([Help: channel URLs](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2657968)).
So Vamorix could become "Spaceman Vam" with a new handle. The Help page doesn't say whether
the old handle is held or freed; [Folklore] reports say YouTube holds it for a short while.

**[Official] Hiding old videos.** Private and unlisted videos drop out of search,
recommendations, subscriber feeds and the channel's Videos tab. Unlisted videos stay
viewable by link and can still sit in a public playlist; private ones can't
([Help: privacy settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/157177)). We found no
official statement that unlisting or privating hurts a channel. Since YouTube judges
each video on its own (section 1), there is no mechanism for harm. Privating is reversible;
deleting is not.

| | Revive Vamorix (rename) | New "Spaceman Vam" channel |
|---|---|---|
| Live-stream eligibility | Probably already verified / past any wait; mobile live needs 50 subs (may already have) | Must verify; first stream may wait 24 h; mobile live locked until 50 subs |
| Old subscribers | Go quiet, don't count against you; a few may come back | None |
| Old content | Private or unlist it; nothing is lost | Old channel stays untouched as an archive |
| Brand | Rename possible; old name lingers in old comments and links | Clean, space-themed from day one |
| Analytics | Old history mixes into lifetime stats (cosmetic) | Clean baseline, easy to read growth |
| Extra cost | None | A second Google/Brand account to manage |

## 3. Raw VODs next to edited videos; live-stream requirements

**[Reported official] Mixing long VODs with edited videos.** We found **no** official YouTube
guidance that tells creators to put streams or VODs on a separate channel, and no statement
that VODs hurt edited uploads. The official principles that do apply:
YouTube judges each video on its own (2020 Q&A); recommendations are pulled per viewer (Beaupré 2026);
and long videos are served where people watch them, e.g. on TVs, without being pushed by
YouTube (Beaupré 2026). The audience test is the tie-breaker: people who watch
3-hour raw runs and people who watch 15-min story cuts may be different groups. That is
the only official reason to split.
**[Folklore]** Many streamers run a "VODs" second channel to keep the main page tidy, and
to keep low-retention VODs from taking up the subscriber notification slots. That last
concern is real given the 3-per-24h notification cap. A simpler fix on one channel: keep
VODs in their own playlist and on the Live tab (streams land there, not on the Videos
tab), and turn off notifications when uploading a raw VOD.

**[Official] Live-stream requirements**
([Help: intro to live streaming](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2474026),
[Help: mobile live](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9228390),
[Help: webcam live](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2853700)):
- Verify the channel (phone verification), and have no live-streaming restrictions in the past 90 days. Minimum age 16.
- "Starting your first live stream may take up to 24 hours" after turning on live streaming;
  after that you can go live right away. **Turn it on a few days before the first planned
  live session.**
- Mobile live needs at least 50 subscribers. Desktop (encoder such as OBS, or webcam) has
  no subscriber minimum. Gameplay from a PC uses the encoder route, so this doesn't block the plan.

## 4. The Vamorix channel itself

**Could not look it up.** `youtube.com/@Vamorix`, `/c/Vamorix` and `/user/Vamorix` all
returned HTTP 404 to our fetcher. A YouTube search page came back empty, Social Blade
returned 403, and a web search found nothing. The 404s may come from the fetcher
(YouTube often blocks automated requests) or from a handle that differs from "Vamorix". Since
YouTube auto-assigned handles in 2022, an old channel may have something like
`@vamorix1234`. **To do by hand:** in YouTube Studio, note the handle, subscriber count, video count,
whether the channel is verified, and whether live streaming is already on (Studio > Create > Go live).

## Recommendation

On the official evidence, **reviving Vamorix has no algorithmic penalty**. Inactive
subscribers just go quiet, old videos can be privated with no known harm, and YouTube
judges each new video on its own. The rename limit (2 per 14 days) makes "Vamorix becomes
Spaceman Vam" a few clicks. The practical win is that live streaming is likely already
unlocked, with no 24 h wait.

The case for a **new channel** is about branding and audience, not the algorithm.
YouTube's own test is "same audience?". If the old Vamorix audience was a different
genre, they were never this series' audience anyway, so the old channel gives you little
beyond a working live setup and a somewhat larger subscriber number. A new channel costs
a 24 h live wait and a second account, nothing more.

Trade-off: **revive and rename** if the old content was broadly gaming or the channel
already has live streaming enabled. It's the lower-friction path, and nothing official
says it hurts. **Start fresh** if you want a clean space-games brand and clean
analytics, and accept starting from 0 subscribers, which official guidance says matters
less than people think. Either way:
- Keep the raw VODs on the same channel, in their own playlist.
- Don't send notifications for the raw VODs, to protect the 3-per-day notification cap.
- Spread uploads across the week.
- Turn on live streaming well before the first live session.

## Decision (2026-11-02)

A new space-games channel under the same Google account. Vamorix stays as it is: the creator won't private or unlist its old videos, and the first run is recorded, so the 24 h live wait doesn't matter. See [concept.md](../concept.md#format).
